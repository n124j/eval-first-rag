Generated by `evals/analysis/failure_taxonomy.py` by replaying the committed recordings offline (guard 0.67, k=3, no model, no network). Every number below is computed by that script; the tests re-run it and compare byte for byte.

## (a) Outcome classes of the 50 answerable items

Each answerable item is in exactly one class. Priority: refused by the guard, refused by the model, valid, no parsable citation, then the first citation problem found (unknown id, short quote, quote not found). Quotes are compared after whitespace normalisation, as the verifier does. For a quote not found, the first matching check wins: quote-character swap, elision (`...`), quote present in another retrieved chunk, case or punctuation only, paraphrase (more than 0.6 of the quote's word tokens occur in the cited chunk), other.

Caveat: the class "differs only in case, spacing or punctuation" is defined by a loose deterministic check (lowercase, remove every non-alphanumeric character, then a substring test). It therefore also catches quotes that are not verbatim and may not support the claim (g015 is an example). The real cause of each difference (for example markup such as backticks or asterisks in the stored chunk text) was NOT individually confirmed.

## (f) What this suggests

Only 15 of 50 answerable items ended with a valid citation. Most of the rest are citation or refusal outcomes; see the retrieval columns in (b). Nothing below was tested; these are readings of the counts, not claims that a fix works.

- Classes that look addressable by the system: quote-character swaps and case, spacing or punctuation differences could be absorbed by a more tolerant comparison, but a looser check also weakens what a valid citation guarantees, so that is a trade-off to decide on purpose. Elided quotes, paraphrases and wrong ids might respond to a different label format or to one citation per sentence. Each would need its own measurement.
- Classes that look like model behaviour: copying a quote word for word from a small quantised model, refusing answerable questions, and answering unanswerable ones from memory. A larger model could change them, which is also untested.
- Of the 35 answerable items without a valid citation, 3 had no expected source in the top 3, so retrieval explains at most 3 of them; the other 32 had an expected source retrieved.
- The dev set (20 items, 12 answerable) gave citation validity 0.67 for the same configuration and the golden set gives 0.30. That is not surprising: with 12 answerable dev items one item moves the rate by 0.08, and the prompt, labels, k and parser were tuned on the 20-item dev set (see docs/adr/0005-prompt-iteration.md), not on the golden items, so some of the dev result reflects fitting to those 20 items. The dev result was a development signal, not a prediction.
