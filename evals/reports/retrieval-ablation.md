# Retrieval ablation (golden set, answerable items)

Generated by `evals/analysis/retrieval_ablation.py` by replaying the committed embeddings (`evals/recordings/embeddings.npz`) offline: no model, no network, default settings (chunk size 200 words, overlap 40). Every number below is computed by that script; the tests re-run it and compare byte for byte.

## Method

- Items: the 50 answerable golden items (40 single-document, 10 multi-document). The 10 unanswerable items have no expected source and are not scored here.
- Scoring is per DOCUMENT, exactly as the harness scores it: a retrieved chunk counts if its source document is an expected source. recall@k is the fraction of an item's expected documents found in the top k chunks (0.5 is possible for a multi item), averaged over items.
- MRR is computed over the top 3 chunks, because the shipped pipeline (k=3) passes only those to the harness.
- Retrievers: keyword-only is the SQLite FTS5 BM25 index (porter tokenizer, query tokens joined by OR); dense-only is cosine similarity over the recorded `nomic-embed-text-v1.5` vectors; hybrid is the shipped `HybridRetriever` (top 50 candidates from each, reciprocal rank fusion with k=60). The retrieval-confidence guard is not applied: it decides whether to answer, not what is retrieved.
- The golden set is used here for measurement only. Nothing was tuned on these numbers.

## Where the retrievers disagree

Success here means at least one expected document is in the top 3 (recall@3 above 0); failure means none is.

- Hybrid fails, dense-only succeeds: g016, g032.
- Dense-only fails, hybrid succeeds: g011.
- Hybrid fails, keyword-only succeeds: g039.
- Keyword-only fails, hybrid succeeds: g019, g023, g027, g047.
- Keyword-only succeeds, dense-only fails: g011, g039.
- Dense-only succeeds, keyword-only fails: g016, g019, g023, g027, g032, g047.

## What this shows

- recall@1: dense-only beats hybrid (0.8100 against 0.8000, difference 0.0100; one single-document item is 0.0200); keyword-only trails hybrid (0.6700).
- recall@3: dense-only ties hybrid (0.8800 against 0.8800, difference 0.0000; one single-document item is 0.0200); keyword-only trails hybrid (0.8200).
- recall@5: dense-only ties hybrid (0.9000 against 0.9000, difference 0.0000; one single-document item is 0.0200); keyword-only trails hybrid (0.8500).
- mrr: dense-only beats hybrid (0.9133 against 0.8967, difference 0.0167; one single-document item is 0.0200); keyword-only trails hybrid (0.7900).
- Counting items with no expected document in the top 3: hybrid recovers 1 dense-only miss(es) (g011) and loses 2 item(s) that dense-only finds (g016, g032).
- Keyword-only finds an expected document for g011, g039, which dense-only misses; it misses 6 items that dense-only finds.
- Plain reading: on this set, adding BM25 to the dense ranking through RRF did not improve document-level retrieval over dense-only; it is level on recall@3 and recall@5 and slightly behind on recall@1 and MRR. The gaps are one or two items, within the noise of 50 items, so this does not show that dense-only is better either. Keyword-only is the weakest of the three on every column.

## Limits of this measurement

- 50 items written by one author. One single-document item moves a recall by 0.02; differences of one or two items are not evidence of anything.
- Document level, not passage level: a chunk from the right page counts even if it does not contain the answer, so these numbers overstate how precisely the answering passage is found.
- One embedding model at one quantisation, one chunking (200/40), one RRF constant (60) and one candidate depth (50). None of these was varied.
